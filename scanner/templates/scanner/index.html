      A LITTLE CLARITY

      Read the label.
      Keep the final say.

      How FoodLens works

      Gemini extracts and translates the label when configured. Without an API key, free open-source Tesseract OCR reads it locally and uses a smaller built-in Dutch–English ingredient dictionary. A separate, conservative rule engine checks the original ingredient names. Neither tool chooses the dietary verdict.

      Three dietary categories. One important fourth answer.

      Vegan-compatible ingredients, vegetarian but not vegan, non-vegetarian ingredient found, or uncertain. An unreadable list, an unfamiliar ingredient or an unresolved source blocks a positive result.

      Privacy by design

      The app removes image metadata before processing. Photos and results are not saved in the app’s database or application logs. With Gemini, the provider processes the photo under its own terms. In local OCR mode, the photo stays on the FoodLens server.

      Saving a scan stores the text result in this browser, not your photo. Anyone using this browser profile may see saved scans. Clear them from the Saved tab. Your dietary setting is stored on this device.

      Not a certification or allergy checker

      FoodLens cannot verify processing aids, manufacturing practices, undeclared ingredients or product certifications. OCR and AI can misread text. Always compare with the original package. “No allergen statement read” never means “allergen-free.”

      Built for the first useful version

      Dutch and English packaging only. One photo per scan. Barcode databases, full on-image translation overlays and recipe recognition are not included in this version.
