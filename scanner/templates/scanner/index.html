How FoodLens works

Gemini extracts and translates the label when configured. Without an API key, free open-source Tesseract OCR reads it locally and uses a smaller built-in Dutch–English ingredient dictionary. A separate, conservative rule engine checks the original ingredient names. Neither tool chooses the dietary verdict.

Four deliberately cautious answers

Vegan-compatible ingredients, vegetarian but not vegan, non-vegetarian ingredient found, or uncertain. An unreadable list, an unfamiliar ingredient, or an unresolved source blocks a positive result.

Privacy by design

The app removes image metadata before processing. Photos and results are not saved in the app database or application logs. With Gemini, the provider processes the photo under its own terms. In local OCR mode, the photo stays on the FoodLens server.

Saved scans, the selected location name, and shopping-list items, quantities, and checked status stay in this browser. Raw coordinates are not stored. Photos are never included. Anyone using this browser profile may see the saved data; clear shopping items and scans from the My List tab.

Not certification or allergy advice

FoodLens cannot verify processing aids, manufacturing practices, undeclared ingredients, or product certifications. OCR and AI can misread text. Always compare with the original package.
